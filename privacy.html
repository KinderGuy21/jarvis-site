Privacy Policy

JARVIS · last updated 23 October 2026

Who this covers

JARVIS is a personal assistant built and used only by Alon Beraha. Its only user is Alon himself, and the only Google account it connects to is his own. It is not offered to the public.

What Google data JARVIS accesses

When Alon signs in with Google, he grants JARVIS these permissions:

  • Gmail (read-only): to search and read his email when he asks about it.
  • Gmail (send): to send an email he has asked it to send. It cannot delete, archive or relabel mail.
  • Google Calendar (events, and calendar list read-only): to read his schedule and create, update or delete events he asks for. It cannot create or delete calendars or change their sharing.
  • Google Drive (read-only): to search and read his files. It cannot create, edit, move, share or delete files.

How the data is used

Google data is used only to answer Alon's own requests: for example reading out an email, summarising his day, or adding a meeting. It is not used for advertising, it is not sold, and it is not used to train general-purpose AI models.

Where the data is kept

JARVIS runs on Alon's own computer. The Google sign-in token is stored there, in a file only his user account can read. Email, calendar and file contents are fetched when a request needs them; short excerpts may be kept in his private local notes and logs on that computer. To answer a request, the relevant text may be processed by the AI model provider JARVIS uses (Anthropic), under that provider's terms, solely to produce the answer.

Sharing

Google user data is not shared with, transferred to or sold to anyone else, except the processing described above that is needed to answer Alon's own request, or where the law requires it.

Google API Services User Data Policy

JARVIS's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access and deleting data

Access can be revoked at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Deleting the token file on the computer running JARVIS also ends its access, and local notes or logs can be deleted there at any time.
